# Human Comms Writing — Standing Defaults

Apply these rules whenever drafting human-facing content: emails, messages, Slack posts, stakeholder updates, cover notes, or any written communication directed at a real person.

These rules set the floor. The `human-comms-writing` skill gives the full structured pass when explicit drafting is requested.

## Core principle

Cut every word that creates distance between writer and reader. Distance language is anything that signals formality over honesty, institution over person, process over thought.

## What to do

**Subject / title:** Name the domain. Signal the pressure level. "AI @ Arksoft — Food for Thought" tells the reader what it is and how urgent it is before they open it.

**Opener:** Strip to the minimum. "Hi team," not "Dear colleagues," not "I hope this finds you well." No warmth theater.

**First sentence:** Lead with the action or the thing, not the sender. "Attaching a deck..." not "I am writing to share..."

**Short descriptors:** Use them to pre-empt the unstated objection. "A short deck" answers "do I have time for this?" before the reader asks it.

**Ownership:** Use "I think" for opinions, not "the data suggests" or "it appears that." Own the view.

**Pressure:** State what you are NOT asking for, before the reader feels the pressure. "Not asking for a decision" pre-empts anxiety. The negation goes first.

**Invitation:** Name the response you want explicitly. "Questions or pushback" beats "feedback if you have any." Naming pushback signals you can handle it.

**Closer:** Two words max. "Feedback welcome." "Let me know." No sign-off theater ("Kind regards", "Please don't hesitate to reach out").

## What to cut

- "I hope this email finds you well" and all variants
- "I wanted to reach out / share / let you know"
- "Please find attached"
- "At your earliest convenience"
- "Do not hesitate to contact me"
- "Kind regards", "Warm regards", "Best wishes"
- Any sentence that restates what the attachment already says
